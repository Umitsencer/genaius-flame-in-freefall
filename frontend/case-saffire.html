Case study · NASA Saffire experiments · 2020–2023

What did the fire do to the air?

After a cargo ship leaves the International Space Station, NASA lit real fires inside it on purpose — with nobody on board — before it burned up in the atmosphere.

We follow one of those fires, Saffire VI-3, from ignition to the selected smoke criterion, alongside the reported changes in the air.

▶ Watch the fire unfold Skip to the key finding
🛰️

An empty spacecraft

A Cygnus cargo ship, far from the station. Free air inside: 16.7 m³ — about one small bedroom.

🔥

One sheet of plastic

A 1 cm-thick sheet of acrylic (PMMA, the clear plastic of display cases), lit by a hot wire.

🌬️

Thin, oxygen-rich air

54.6 kPa (about half of sea-level pressure) with 30.3 % oxygen — Earth air has 21 %. NASA calls this an exploration atmosphere.

Step 2 · The key finding

Elapsed from ignition: 5 min 44 s to the selected smoke criterion.

And by the time it did, the authors note, much of the harmful gas had already been released.

Gas values are increases measured near one sensor in an empty spacecraft, compared by the NASA authors with crew limits. They are not what a person would have breathed.

Step 4 · Why this matters

Catching smoke in space is harder than it sounds.

🫧

The smoke is tiny

In Saffire IV–V, most of the smoke mass was in particles smaller than 1 micrometre (PM1) — dozens of times thinner than a human hair.

Fortenberry, NTRS 20220009167

🔍

No detector sees everything

On the space station, the light-based smoke detector often missed the small particles from Teflon and Kapton — materials common in electronics.

SAME / SAME-2 on the ISS (NIST 2018; AIAA 2012-3536)

❓

The open question

Could watching CO and CO₂ give an earlier warning than smoke alone? These results raise that question. They do not answer it.

Our reading — not a NASA conclusion

Step 5 · What this does not tell us

We say what we don't know.

👤

No crew was on board. This is not a crew-exposure or survival assessment.

📍

Increases near one sensor. Not the full air a person would breathe in a cabin.

∅

V-3 and V-4 have no published gas data. We show them as gaps, never as zero.

🌙

Microgravity is not the Moon or Mars. These results are not carried over to partial gravity.
